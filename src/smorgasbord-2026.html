Featured classes

"The Daly Jump" Charleston with Jay Sheaffer

Jay choreographed a routine to Count Basie's 1960 recording of "The Daly Jump," which features Cincinnati's own Frank Foster on tenor sax. It's taught as two classes.

  • Solo Charleston
  • All levels, fast-paced

The Daly Jump: Solo Charleston

Jay Sheaffer

An eight-eight solo routine built almost entirely from Charleston combos, variations and rhythms, bookended by a boogie drop and a Shorty George. It's a choreography class with an improv goal: Jay shows how he slices and dices 1920s and '30s Charleston into seamless improv, and pushes you to break your solo dancing into pieces smaller than a full eight-count move.

Level: open to everyone, but it moves fast. Some solo Charleston experience is recommended.

  • Partner Charleston
  • Intermediate/Advanced

The Daly Jump: Partner Charleston

Jay Sheaffer & Sam Uebel

Eight eights of partnered Charleston choreography to the same tune. You'll learn led turning transitions that take you from side-by-side to tandem to hand-to-hand and back to side-by-side, all moves you can bring straight to the social floor. If the class is ready for it, we'll perform the routine at the evening dance.

Level: Intermediate/Advanced. Previous partner Charleston experience required.

Cincinnati jazz history

About Frank Foster

Frank Foster got his start at 16, playing in a dance band in Cincinnati's Walnut Hills neighborhood in 1942. He studied at Wilberforce University, since the Cincinnati Conservatory didn't accept students of color at the time. After serving in WWII, he played and wrote for Count Basie from 1953 to 1964, including "Shiny Stockings" (1955).

"Cincinnati was almost like a sort of crossroads for big bands coming back and forth across the country. There was an establishment called the Coliseum, which was a big dancehall. All the big bands came through and played there."

Frank Foster

Instructors

Who's teaching

  • Jesse FatherreeSwinGallery, Cincinnati

    SwinGallery, Cincinnati

    Jesse Fatherree

    Jesse got introduced to swing dance in Cincinnati in 1998 and jumped in head first from the start. After being inspired by classes with Frankie Manning, Ryan Francois and Kevin St Laurent, he quickly began to travel from coast to coast to learn and compete.

    His most challenging dance success was the 2003 Heart Health Hop, where 42 participants danced for 52 hours and 3 minutes to set a Guinness World Record for the longest dance party marathon.

    Jesse is one of the original founders of the SwinGallery, Ohio's longest-running weekly swing dance. Since 2001 he's been teaching weekly classes and regional workshops in Lindy Hop, Charleston, Balboa, Blues and East Coast Swing. Over the years, he's also been into West Coast Swing, Salsa and, more recently, Argentine Tango.
  • Samantha (Sam) UebelSwinGallery, Cincinnati

    SwinGallery, Cincinnati

    Samantha (Sam) Uebel

    Sam loved dance from a very early age, when she aspired to be a Fly Girl on the '90s sketch comedy series In Living Color. #CareerGoals!

    She fell in love with swing dance in college in 2007, where she got her start in the Louisville scene. She then moved to Nashville, then Cincinnati. Sam now dances and teaches regularly at SwinGallery, Cincinnati's longest-running Lindy Hop event (every Thursday).

    Her favorite styles of dance are Blues, Balboa and Lindy Hop, and she loves building the dance community.

    Chicago

    Jay Sheaffer

    Jay proudly represents Chicago's South Side, where they study contemporary Black American dance forms, including House and Chicago Steppin'. Their extensive solo dance training informs their movement, musicality, and approach to partner dancing, grounded in the groove and cultural roots of House dance and House music.

    In the Spring of 2025, Jay joined the teaching team at Traffic Jam! Swing, where they have continued to develop their swing dance practice alongside John Holmstrom, Kerry Kapaku & Bob Bednarz. They also teach with the Fizz and Bluetopia teams and remain connected to the Chicago Lindy Hop community and beyond.

    As a switch dancer, Jay enjoys exploring the possibilities of dancing both roles, bringing curiosity and a nuanced understanding of partner dynamics to the social dance floor and the classroom.

Good to know

Before you come

  • No partner required Partners rotate in class.
  • Some experience helps Swing background is helpful but not required. Get a head start at a Thursday night beginner lesson.
  • Snacks provided Meals are on your own. The Hoffner Pub is at street level in the same building.
  • Dress to move Wear something comfortable. Vintage is fun but never required.

Getting there

Parking & bus

  • Free lot

    The lot behind Northside Bank & Trust (across Hamilton, on Palm Ave) is usually open outside banking hours.

  • Free street parking

    On the side streets, about half a block off Hamilton Ave.

  • Hamilton Ave meters run until 9 pm

    Meters are enforced 8 am–9 pm, Monday through Saturday, including Saturday, Nov 28 (the day after Thanksgiving isn't a meter holiday). If you park on Hamilton, feed the meter until 9.

  • Public lots

    Northside has several public lots near the Lodge (managed with Star Parking).

  • Bus

    Metro routes 15X, 16, 17, 19, 20, 27 and 51 serve Northside (go-metro.com).
